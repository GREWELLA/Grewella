============================================================ 4. DESIGN SYSTEM AND RESPONSIVE LAYOUT ============================================================

Body: Inter or another highly readable sans-serif.

Desktop hero heading: approximately 64–88 px.

Mobile hero heading: approximately 40–52 px.

Section headings: approximately 34–54 px desktop and 29–38 px mobile.

Use fluid typography such as clamp() where useful.

Layout:



Maximum content width: approximately 1,240–1,320 px.

Desktop side padding: 48–64 px; tablet around 32 px; mobile 20–24 px.

Use a 12-column desktop grid where appropriate.

Establish consistent spacing tokens and readable line lengths.

Buttons should be comfortably tappable, usually at least 48 px tall.

Use rounded corners purposefully, not on every element.

Do not repeat the same card layout in every section.

Create reusable components for navigation, buttons, sections, forms, cards, pricing, project previews, accordions, privacy dialogs, and the footer.

============================================================ 7. MOTION DESIGN THROUGHOUT THE WEBSITE ============================================================



Create a consistent motion language in which each animation has a purpose.



Page entrance: short staggered text reveals, smooth object entrances, no long preloader. Scroll storytelling: controlled section reveals, subtle depth, and selective scroll-linked transformations; keep text readable and scrolling predictable. Micro-interactions: buttons respond to hover, focus, press, and tap; cards may lift or tilt subtly; links get a clear animated underline or color transition; forms show loading, success, and error states; accordions animate accessibly. 3D and effects: carefully controlled lighting, shadows, dimensional typography used sparingly, and selective morphing shapes, fluid transitions, animated gradients, or particles where they support the story.



Suggested starting ranges: micro-interactions 150–300 ms; section reveals 300–650 ms; hero entrance 600–1,200 ms. Adjust after testing. Respect prefers-reduced-motion and remove nonessential motion without hiding content or functionality. Avoid constant distracting movement, flashing, or effects that block reading.

B. BOOK A CONSULTATION / APPOINTMENT FORM

Offer a separate “Book a Consultation” CTA for visitors who want to discuss a project. Include name, business name (optional), preferred contact details, meeting type (phone/video/in-person only if genuinely available), project topic, preferred date, and available time slot.



Do not show fictional availability. If a real scheduling integration is not configured, let the visitor submit preferred times and clearly explain that the appointment is not confirmed until Gruvella confirms it. If an actual booking system is connected, show only available slots and confirm the booking only after the system reports success. Provide clear rescheduling/cancellation instructions if appointments can be booked.



For both forms:

- Provide an appropriate privacy notice before collecting personal information.

- Obtain affirmative consent where consent is the applicable legal basis.

- Keep optional marketing consent separate.

- Validate and sanitize input server-side.

- Protect against spam and automated abuse.

- Use HTTPS and keep secrets server-side.

- Avoid public storage of customer details.

- Configure notifications only through actual integrations.

- Do not claim success until the backend confirms it.

- Define appropriate retention and deletion practices.



If the backend or scheduling system is not configured, clearly identify it as not connected during development. Do not present localStorage-only forms as production systems.
